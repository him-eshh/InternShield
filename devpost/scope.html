InternShield

Paste a recruiter message and get an evidence-based scam-confidence report. If it holds up, find the real posting and act on it.

The kernel: evidence, not opinion

  1. The AI extracts facts (company, role, pay, what they're asking you to do, which app they want to move you to, pressure tactics). It never decides the verdict.
  2. Fixed rules score them. They're deterministic, can't hallucinate, and can't be manipulated by instructions hidden in a scam message.
  3. The AI explains the result in plain English, after the score is set.
  4. The real posting is cross-checked as evidence, not proof, because scammers copy real postings. A missing posting is neutral.
  5. It never says "safe." It says "no red flags found" and always links the official careers page.

The core loop

The first visit asks for field, level, and skills. That profile stays in the browser: no login, nothing stored on a server.

Who it's for

Students and job seekers in any field who get recruiter messages on Handshake, LinkedIn, email, or text. They can no longer tell AI-polished scams from real offers, so they risk getting scammed or start ignoring real offers too.

Boundary: now, later, cut

Core: the PoC finish line
  • First-visit profile form (field, level, skills), saved in the browser
  • Resume upload, saved in the browser (feeds the level check now and the Tier 2 tailored resume)
  • Paste the message, sender contact, and claimed company
  • AI fact extraction (AI extracts and explains, never scores)
  • ruleSender domain vs. the company's real domain
  • ruleFree email (like Gmail) from someone claiming to be a recruiter
  • ruleDomain registered recently
  • ruleAsking for money, sending a check, or asking you to buy equipment
  • ruleMoving you to WhatsApp, Telegram, or text-only interviews
  • ruleRole far above the user's level (resume + profile level)
  • ruleField-specific scam patterns (defined in the PRD)
  • Recruiter web search: public profile found or not, with the link (information only)
  • Posting cross-check: does the pay or role match, does the sender belong to the company, does the contact method match how the posting says to apply? Not found counts as neutral.
  • Animated score ring, evidence cards (green good, red bad), plain-English explanation, official link
Tier 2: tailored resume + application tracker
  • Tailored resume PDF from the uploaded resume. Select a section to request changes. It only rewords real experience and never makes anything up.
  • Tracker board for each offer: saved, applied, interviewing
Tier 3: mock interview (everyone + tech)
  • Recruiter screen and behavioral round for everyone
  • Tech: live coding in an in-browser Python/JS editor, plus system design on a drawing board. Meant to be the standout.
Tier 4: other fields' rounds
  • Business: case interviews · Finance: technical questions · Design and arts: portfolio walkthrough + design challenge
Explicitly cut
  • The AI deciding the verdict: it can hallucinate, and scammers could hide instructions in the message to manipulate it.
  • Calling any message "safe": no tool can guarantee that.
  • Login, accounts, and server storage: not needed. The profile and resume stay in the browser.

Feel

Clean and calm, with lots of white space and one big headline, like Apple's product pages. The score fills in as a ring, evidence cards appear one at a time, transitions are smooth, and it looks good on a phone.

Why it matters

Himesh is hunting for a summer internship himself. Scams have become realistic enough that people ignore real offers. InternShield is meant to stop that.
