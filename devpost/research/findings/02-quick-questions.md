*Research for brief 02. Prepared Thu Oct 22, 2026, about 1:00–2:30 AM ET. Every link below was opened, or the endpoint was called live from the box, during this session unless it is marked **[unverified]**. All example messages are made up.*

## 0. TL;DR (read this if nothing else)

1. **Ask about the decision, not the score.** Today a question is shown if *any* answer moves the score by *any* amount. Change it to: show a question only if one of its answers could **change the level** (low/medium/high), **turn a critical flag on**, or **change the advice** (recovery steps). That is the value-of-information idea (Howard 1966) applied the same way doctors use "test thresholds" (Pauker & Kassirer 1980). It cuts noise and makes every question worth answering. Cap the box at **3 questions**.
2. **Replace 2 of the 4 questions and add 2 that matter more.** The highest-value unknowns in FTC, FBI, Scamwatch, LinkedIn and Handshake guidance are: *did you apply first*, *have they asked you to pay, deposit, install or share anything* (often off-text, e.g. on a call), *have you talked live*, and *have you already acted* (which switches the report to recovery). Drop "Is the employer outside the US?". The evidence doesn't support softening chat-app contact by country.
3. **Fix the company-name bug with layered rules, not a bigger model.** Use a department/team blocklist, a "the ___ team" pattern, organisation-suffix scoring, a signature-block parser, a sender-domain cross-check and a strict-JSON LLM field `isDepartment`. A live test on the box found that compromise.js also tags "Talent Acquisition group" as an organisation, so off-the-shelf NER alone won't fix it.
4. **Resolve the official domain for free before spending Tavily credits.** Try Wikidata first (`wbsearchentities`, then P856 "official website"; no key; 200 req/min for browser traffic). Then Clearbit Autocomplete (still answers today but undocumented, so use it only as a fallback). Use Tavily last. Then **detect the careers-page ATS** and query the public Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and SmartRecruiters job-board APIs (no key) to confirm the role really exists.
5. **Budget bug:** the Tavily free plan is **1,000 credits a month** ([Tavily docs](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-credits)). The site cap of 80 searches a day would use that up in about 12 days, which could fall right in the judging window (Oct 27–30). Gate searches by value of information and lower the daily cap.
6. **"I don't know" should stay score-neutral** (that's correct). Add a one-line *range* hint when unanswered items could flip the level, and turn each "Couldn't check" line into a do-it-yourself step that links to the official place to check.
7. **UI:** keep a single card, but move it **below the explanation** and title it "Make this check more accurate · optional". Add a "could change" hint under the ring that links to the card. Show the top question open and the others behind "+2 more". Put the "Already acted?" recovery entry point in its own card.

## 3. Answers to brief §7, numbered

### 3.1 (Q1) What should we ask, and how? The 3–6 highest-value questions

Value of information for *our* rule set is highest where (a) the answer can't be found automatically and (b) an answer can flip the level or a critical flag. Ranked:

| Rank | id | Exact wording (≤ 9 words) | Options (short for phones) | Why-we-ask line (shown small) | Why it's high value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `asked_to_do` | **"Have they asked you to do any of these?"** (multi-select) | Pay or buy something · Deposit a check · Send crypto / "top up" · Install or run a file · Share SSN or bank info · **None yet** · Not sure | "These often happen on a call or in a later chat, not in the first message." | Any "yes" is a critical flag (score ≥ 85). It covers off-text asks (FBI: deposits come *after* "training"). |
| 2 | `who_first` | **"Did you apply for this job first?"** | Yes, I applied · No, they found me · Not sure | "Scammers often say they 'found your resume.'" | Drives `applied_first` (−10) and enables the unsolicited signals. Replaces `source`. |
| 3 | `channel` (only if it can't be inferred) | **"Where did this message reach you?"** | Email · LinkedIn · Handshake · Text / WhatsApp · Other | "Real employers rarely start on WhatsApp." | Drives `surprise_text` (+25). Often inferable (§3.8), so it's usually hidden. |
| 4 | `talked_live` | **"Have you talked to them live?"** | Video call · Phone call · Only chat/text · Not yet | "Real hiring usually includes a live interview." | "Only chat/text" → chat-only interview (+20). "Not yet" plus offer language → `no_interview` (+20). |
| 5 | `signed_offer` (keep) | **"Have you signed an offer letter from them?"** | Yes · No | "After signing, HR portals ask for tax and bank details." | The existing stage-aware SSN logic. Wording tightened ("from them"). |
| 6 | `already_acted` (recovery; not scored) | **"Have you already done any of these?"** | Sent money · Deposited a check · Shared SSN/ID/bank · Installed something · No | "If yes, we'll show what to do first." | Changes the **advice**, not the score. Always worth asking when the level is medium or high. |

Wording rules applied: second person, verbs first, no jargon ("ATS", "off-platform"), ≤ 9 words, options of 1–3 words, and "Not sure" instead of "I don't know" (shorter, and less like admitting a failure). GOV.UK's pattern research supports one clear question per unit and short labels ([GOV.UK question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/); [GOV.UK structuring forms](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure)). NN/g recommends a brief justification for any unexpected question ([NN/g EAS](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/eas-framework-simplify-forms/); [NN/g sensitive questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sensitive-questions/)).

**Ask vs automate (from the brief's §6 list):**
- "Did you reach out first?" → **ask** (`who_first`).
- "Pay or deposit a check?" → **ask** (`asked_to_do`).
- "Is the recruiter's email the same as on the company's site?" → **automate.** We already compare the sender domain to the official domain. Don't push this to the user.
- "Video, phone or chat?" → **ask** (`talked_live`).
- "Check, equipment, or install link?" → merged into `asked_to_do`.
- "Have you shared personal info yet?" → **ask** (`already_acted`), routing to recovery.

### 3.2 (Q2) Choosing questions: policy, ranking, cap, adaptivity

**Verdict: "Ask only if an answer changes the score" is the right *instinct* but the wrong *target*.** Value-of-information theory says information is worth gathering only if it can change the **decision** ([Howard 1966, *Information Value Theory*, IEEE TSSC, doi:10.1109/TSSC.1966.300074](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSSC.1966.300074)). Medicine's threshold approach orders a test only when the pre-test probability lies between "no action" and "treat" thresholds ([Pauker & Kassirer 1980, NEJM, doi:10.1056/NEJM198005153022003](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM198005153022003)). Our "decision" is the **level** plus the **advice block**, not the raw number.

**Proposed `pickQuestions` v2 (deterministic, about 40 lines):**
```
for q in relevantUnanswered:
  for a in q.options (excluding "Not sure"):
    s_a = score(with answer a); L_a = level(s_a)
  value(q) = Σ_a w(a) · [L_a ≠ L_now]          // level flip
           + 1.0 · [any a adds a critical]       // critical can switch on
           + 1.0 · [q is already_acted and L_now ≥ medium]   // advice flip
  // w(a): hand-set priors. Start uniform. Never learned from users (no storage).
show top 3 by value(q) > 0, ties broken by the fixed order in §3.1
```
- **Cap at 3.** Web-survey research shows that a longer questionnaire lowers both starting and completion rates and reduces answer quality on later items ([Galesic & Bosnjak 2009, *Public Opinion Quarterly*, doi:10.1093/poq/nfp031](https://doi.org/10.1093/poq/nfp031)). Three short tap-questions is a defensible ceiling. **[Inference: no study exists for scam checkers specifically.]**
- **Adaptive, but gently.** Computerized adaptive testing picks the next item based on prior answers and stops early when more items wouldn't change the estimate ([Weiss & Kingsbury 1984, *J. Educ. Meas.*, doi:10.1111/j.1745-3984.1984.tb01040.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3984.1984.tb01040.x)). Apply it as: after each answer, **re-run `pickQuestions` for unanswered items, and only remove questions that became moot, never add new ones**. That keeps the "no layout jump" fix from last night while avoiding pointless questions. Fade removed items out with "No longer needed."
- **Expected score change vs information gain:** with a hand-built rule engine and no outcome data, entropy-based information gain has nothing calibrated to work from. Level-flip counting is explainable ("this could move you to *Strong signs*") and testable.

### 3.4 (Q4) Phone numbers: what we can responsibly infer

Tested live on the box with **libphonenumber-js/max**:

| Input (fictional/reserved) | country | getType() |
|---|---|---|
| +1 415 555 2671 | US | `FIXED_LINE_OR_MOBILE` |
| +1 800 555 0199 | US | `TOLL_FREE` |
| +44 20 7946 0958 | GB | `FIXED_LINE` |
| +91 98765 43210 | IN | `MOBILE` |
| +234 803 123 4567 | NG | `MOBILE` |

- **Country:** yes, from the calling code. That's where the number is *registered*, not where the person is.
- **Toll-free / premium:** yes. `getType()` needs the **max** metadata (an extra ~65 kB) and returns `undefined` for most countries on the default min build ([libphonenumber-js README](https://github.com/catamphetamine/libphonenumber-js)).
- **Mobile vs landline in the US/Canada:** **no.** NANP ranges come back as `FIXED_LINE_OR_MOBILE` (above), and Google's FAQ explains that ambiguous ranges get that type and that numbers can be ported between carriers ([libphonenumber FAQ](https://github.com/google/libphonenumber/blob/master/FAQ.md)).
- **VoIP:** a `VOIP` type exists only where a country has dedicated VoIP ranges. US VoIP numbers (Google Voice, TextNow) are **not** detectable from the number. That would need a paid carrier lookup, which was correctly cut in the PRD.
- **Short codes (5–6 digits):** detect them with a simple regex in the sender field. US short codes go through **registry vetting of the registrant's legal identity plus separate per-carrier campaign approval** ([US Short Code Registry vetting](https://www.usshortcodes.com/registry-vetting-process/); [Short Code Registry FAQs](https://www.usshortcodes.com/learn-more/faqs/)). So a short code points to a registered business sender, such as ATS interview-scheduling texts. It is not proof, though: carriers note scam texts also come from short codes ([Verizon](https://www.verizon.com/about/account-security/smishing-and-spam-text-messages)). **Use: info only.** No good-sign points.
- **FTC/FCC on long codes:** the FTC tells people to ignore unexpected job texts and WhatsApp messages and to verify through the company's own site or number ([FTC Nov 2024](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/11/random-text-offering-you-job-its-probably-scam); [FTC top text scams 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/04/top-text-scams-2024)). In the FTC's hand-coded sample, job opportunities were among the top five text scams of 2024, and reported losses to text scams reached $470M ([FTC press release, Apr 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/new-ftc-data-show-top-text-message-scams-2024-overall-losses-text-scams-hit-470-million)). The FCC's guide covers consent rules for autodialed texts and "forward to 7726", but says nothing about long codes vs short codes for jobs ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts)). **[No FTC/FCC statement specific to long vs short codes for job texts was found.]**
- **Is "employer abroad = chat apps OK" defensible?** **Only partly.** WhatsApp is a common *candidate-preferred* first-contact channel in some markets. In Mexico, OCC's survey says 58% of candidates prefer WhatsApp for first contact ([RRHH Digital, Mar 2025](https://www.rrhhdigital.mx/secciones/seleccion/3985/WhatsApp-es-el-canal-favorito-de-los-candidatos-para-el-primer-contacto-laboral)). In Brazil, a 2020 report says companies using WhatsApp for interviews and hiring rose 53% ([TI Inside](https://tiinside.com.br/en/14/10/2020/employment-contracts-via-WhatsApp-have-increased-by-53-since-the-start-of-the-pandemic/)) **[secondary source]**. India: **[no primary source found]**. Against that, Australia's Scamwatch lists recruiter contact via WhatsApp/Signal/Telegram as a warning sign even in a high-WhatsApp country ([Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/types-of-scams/jobs-and-employment-scams)), and task-scam crews run from compounds abroad ([FBI Operation Level Up](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/victim-services/national-crimes-and-victim-resources/operation-level-up)), so "abroad" also correlates with scams. **Recommendation:** soften `off_platform` (20 → 10) **only when the user applied first** (`who_first = yes`) and no money or data was asked, **regardless of country**. Delete the `outside_us` question and the `+NN` inference.

### 3.5 (Q5) "I don't know" handling

- **Keep it score-neutral (current behavior is right).** Not knowing is not evidence either way. Survey research also shows "don't know" options can invite low-effort answering ([Krosnick et al., *POQ* 2002, doi:10.1086/341394](https://doi.org/10.1086/341394)). That is a reason to make "Not sure" visually secondary (last, plain text style), **not** a reason to remove it, because a forced guess would be worse for a scam tool.
- **Add a range hint instead of a confidence penalty.** When any unanswered or "Not sure" question has `value(q) > 0`, show one line under the ring: *"Could change to Strong signs depending on 2 answers ↓"*. This is the honest "uncertainty band": it's computed exactly from the rule engine, it's explainable, and it isn't fake precision. Stripe Radar's docs make the same point: when key details are missing, Radar "can't compute all of the data it needs to accurately evaluate each payment" ([Stripe risk insights](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/reviews/risk-insights)).
- **Keep the coverage line** ("9 of 14 checks ran"). It is the confidence indicator. Don't add a second one.
- **Make "Couldn't check" actionable.** Each line gets a do-it-yourself step and an official link, e.g. "Couldn't confirm the job on their site → Open <official domain>/careers". Google Security Checkup orders items by importance and uses a status icon per item (green = fine, yellow/red = action) ([Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/46526)) **[icon colors per search summary, page JS-rendered]**. Copy that: neutral gray "?" icon, never red, for unknowns.

### 3.6 (Q6) Where the user is in the scam: recovery mode

**Yes.** `already_acted` should switch on a **Recovery card above the evidence** (the report still shows). Order the steps by time-sensitivity, using the official sources:

1. **Stop:** stop replying and stop sending money. "Scammers will keep asking for more money until you stop" ([Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/stop-check-protect/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed); [FBI crypto job scams](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/victim-services/national-crimes-and-victim-resources/cryptocurrency-job-scams)).
2. **Sent money:** contact the company you paid through **immediately** and ask for a reversal. Branch by method: bank/Zelle, payment app, wire, card, gift card (keep the card and receipt), crypto exchange/ATM, cash by mail (USPIS 1-877-876-2455) ([FTC: What to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)).
3. **Deposited a check:** don't spend or send any of the funds, and contact your bank now. Banks can claw the money back ([Fed Pause & Verify](https://fedpaymentsimprovement.org/wp-content/uploads/pause-and-verify-a-guide-for-individuals-to-avoid-fake-check-scams.pdf); [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/i-cashed-a-check-at-my-bankcredit-union-but-later-the-check-was-found-to-be-fraudulent-do-i-have-to-repay-the-bankcredit-union-en-989/); [FTC fake checks](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-spot-avoid-and-report-fake-check-scams)).
4. **Shared SSN:** if it was used, go to [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/) for a recovery plan. If you're not sure, go to IdentityTheft.gov/databreach ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)). **Freeze your credit** at all 3 bureaus (free) and/or place a 1-year fraud alert ([FTC credit freezes & fraud alerts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/credit-freezes-and-fraud-alerts); [FTC recovery steps](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/09/how-recover-identity-theft)).
5. **Shared bank login or passwords:** change the password and turn on 2FA ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)).
6. **Installed something:** update and run security software, remove what it finds, and change passwords and turn on 2FA ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)). Add a line *(our inference)* to change passwords from a different, clean device.
7. **Report:** [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/); [IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/) (the FBI asks for transaction details, per the [IC3 PSA](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240604)); report inside the platform (Handshake "Report" in the inbox, [Handshake](https://support.joinhandshake.com/hc/en-us/articles/34476362391831-How-to-Spot-and-Report-Scams-on-Handshake)); tell your campus career center. VT's page says to report "even if you are not certain" ([VT CPD](https://career.vt.edu/resources/protect-yourself-from-employment-scams/)).
8. **Watch for follow-up scams:** never pay a "recovery" service ([IC3 PSA](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240604); [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/stop-check-protect/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed)).

Tone: Scamwatch opens with "there's no shame in getting scammed". Use a calm, non-judgmental line like that.

### 3.8 (Q8) Better company and sender extraction

**Live test on the box (fictional text):** *"Hi Alex, I'm Jordan Lee from the University Recruiting team at Brightline Data Solutions. Our Talent Acquisition group would like to schedule a call. Best, Jordan Lee, Campus Recruiter, Brightline Data Solutions Inc."*
- compromise.js `organizations()` → `['Brightline Data', 'Talent Acquisition group', 'Brightline Data Solutions Inc.']`. That's a truncated name **plus a department**.
- On "Jordan Lee from the University Recruiting team reached out…", compromise found **nothing**.
- wink-nlp + `wink-eng-lite-web-model` returned **no entities**. The lite model doesn't do organisation NER **[inference from output and model scope]**.
- transformers.js NER (`Xenova/bert-base-NER`, MIT-licensed base `dslim/bert-base-NER`) works in the browser, but the ONNX weights are **≈94–109 MB** (q4f16 / int8) to **431 MB** (fp32) (checked on the Hugging Face API). That's too heavy for a phone, and it still tags departments as ORG. ❌
- Signature parsers: **Talon** (Mailgun, Python, Apache-2.0, active) and **email-reply-parser** (GitHub's Ruby original; Crisp's JS port, MIT) strip quoted replies and signatures, but they don't label the company. Useful at most to *isolate* the signature block. JS port: [crisp-oss/email-reply-parser](https://github.com/crisp-oss/email-reply-parser); [mailgun/talon](https://github.com/mailgun/talon) (Python, server-only). 🟡

**Recommended pipeline (deterministic first, LLM confirms, about 1 day):**
1. **Isolate the signature:** take the last 2–8 non-empty lines after a sign-off (`Best|Regards|Thanks|Sincerely|Cheers|—|--`), or after the sender's name appears for the second time.
2. **Generate candidates** from (a) signature lines, (b) "at/with/join X", (c) "X's (Recruiting|Talent) team" (possessive = company), (d) URLs/emails in the text → registrable domain label (tldts), (e) gazetteer hits (EDGAR names plus a short list of the ~200 most common student employers, hand-written).
3. **Reject departments:** a candidate is a department if its head noun, after stripping "the/our/your", is in `{Recruiting, Recruitment, Talent, Talent Acquisition, Acquisition, Human Resources, HR, People, People Ops, People Operations, Campus, University, Early Careers, Early Talent, Emerging Talent, Careers, Hiring, Staffing, Onboarding, Payroll, Team, Group, Department, Office, Division}`, **or** if it appears in "the X team/department/group/office". That fixes "University Recruiting" and "Talent Acquisition group".
4. **Score candidates:** +3 if it ends with a legal/org suffix (`Inc|LLC|Ltd|Corp|Corporation|Co\.|GmbH|PLC|LLP|S\.A\.|Pvt|Technologies|Solutions|Labs|Systems|Consulting|Partners|Capital|Health|Bank`); +3 if its normalized form ≈ the sender email's domain label (Levenshtein ≤ 2 or prefix); +2 if it's in the signature; +2 if it's a gazetteer hit; +1 per repeat mention; −5 if it's a known job board/ATS/platform name (LinkedIn, Handshake, Indeed, Greenhouse…, since scammers mention them). Prefer the **longest** candidate that contains another, so "Brightline Data Solutions Inc." beats "Brightline Data".
5. **Cross-check with the LLM.** The extraction already runs. Add fields to the Groq **strict** JSON schema (supported for `openai/gpt-oss-20b` and `-120b` per [Groq Structured Outputs](https://console.groq.com/docs/structured-outputs)): `company: {name, quote, source: "signature"|"body"|"email_domain"|"link", isDepartmentOrTeam: boolean}`. Accept the LLM value only if its quote verifies word for word **and** it passes step 3. If the rule winner and the LLM disagree, prefer the one that matches the sender domain. Otherwise show the rule winner with an "Is this the company?" chip.
6. **Disambiguate** with Wikidata (company-type items only) or EDGAR before Tavily. If several matches exist, use the one whose official domain equals the sender's registrable domain. Otherwise don't claim an official domain ("Couldn't confirm which company this is").
7. **Sender:** use `findPhoneNumbersInText(text, 'US')` from libphonenumber-js (a port of Google's PhoneNumberMatcher) instead of the US-only regex. Detect a 5–6-digit short code as its own sender type. For screenshots, ask the vision model to also return `appGuess: "whatsapp"|"imessage_sms"|"linkedin"|"gmail_outlook"|"handshake"|"unknown"` from the UI chrome. Pre-select the channel with a "from screenshot" tag, so `channel` is rarely asked.
8. **Tests:** add about 20 fictional signature variants to Vitest (department-first, possessive, "Inc." vs none, free-mail plus a named company, staffing agency "on behalf of", a non-English sign-off).
